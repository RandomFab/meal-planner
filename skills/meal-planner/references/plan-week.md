# Planning the week

Contents: 1. Which week · 2. Read first · 3. Questions of the week · 4. Composition rules · 5. Servings and prep ahead · 6. Showing the menu · 7. Writing to Notion · 8. Never

## 1. Which week

The menu covers 7 days, starting on the profile's "First day of the menu". By default, plan the next week that has no Menu lines yet. If the user names a week or days ("redo next week", "fill Thursday to Sunday"), plan only those.

## 2. Read first

1. **Household profile**: every section. Journal lines with an end date ("until …") apply only until that date.
2. **Dishes**: all except Banned. Note Status, Type, Meal, Total time, Season, Main protein, Tags, Ingredients filled.
3. **Menu, last 3 weeks**: what was eaten, feedback, and dishes that should come back soon: lines Postponed or Skipped, and Notes "Postponed: <dish>".
4. **Products**, only if the stock module is on: products Opened or Low, to use first.
5. **Dish ingredients**: which dishes use the products to use first.

## 3. Questions of the week

Ask once, in one short message, only what you cannot know:

- Changes this week: meals out, guests, days away.
- Lunchbox days, if the profile says they vary from week to week.
- If the stock module is off: "Anything in the fridge to use up?"

Give your defaults in the same message ("If you don't answer, I'll plan every meal at home"). When there is no answer (scheduled run), plan with the defaults and list your assumptions at the top of the menu.

## 4. Composition rules

**Frame**

- Plan the meals listed in the profile section "Meals", for each day of the week.
- Rituals come first: place them, then fill the rest.
- Use-up days (profile "Week"): simple dishes from what is left at home. No fresh product from the coming shopping.
- Time: lunch ≤ weekday lunch time, dinner ≤ weekday dinner time, weekend meals ≤ weekend time (profile "Time").

**Leftovers and lunchbox**

- A dinner tagged Good for leftovers can feed the next day's lunch: that lunch line gets Status Leftovers, no Dish, and Note "Leftovers of <dish>". Use this when it saves cooking time.
- For each lunchbox day D: dinner on D−1 is a dish tagged Good for leftovers, with Extra for lunchbox checked and extra servings (section 5). Lunch on D is a line with Status Lunchbox, no Dish, Note "From <dish>".

**Restrictions and tastes**

- Never a Banned dish. Never a dish containing anything in "Never". Avoid "Dislikes" unless the dish has an adaptation the user approved.
- Follow "Protein habits" and "Likes". Favourites come back more often than Approved dishes.
- Season: only dishes whose Season includes the current season, or All year. Use the season where the user lives; if unknown, assume the northern hemisphere (spring = March to May, summer = June to August, autumn = September to November, winter = December to February) and say so once.

**Variety** (profile "Variety")

- Never the same dish two days in a row.
- Everyday dishes: no more often than the profile allows (default once a week each).
- Special dishes: check their last date in Menu; respect the profile's interval (default every two weeks).
- Vary Main protein across the week.

**New dishes**

- Place as many new dishes as the profile asks (default 1 per week): a To try dish first; if there is none, suggest a new recipe that fits the profile and add it only if the user agrees, as a full recipe with Status To try.
- Put a new dish on an evening without constraint: not before a lunchbox day, not on a busy day the user mentioned.

**Stock** (stock module on)

- Prefer dishes that use products Opened or Low, fresh products first.

**Dishes without ingredients** (Ingredients filled = no)

- They can be planned. Mention them once under the menu: their ingredients will be needed for the shopping list.

**Sides**

- Never add a side to a dish yourself. If a meal looks incomplete, ask in the presentation ("The quiche would be more complete with a salad, shall I add it to the recipe?").

## 5. Servings and prep ahead

- Servings = sum of the appetites of the members eating that meal (profile "Household"), plus one appetite per lunchbox to prepare. Show it in words ("2 people + 1 lunchbox").
- If the amount to cook is not obvious, say it in the Note in simple units ("⅓ pack of pasta in total").
- Prep ahead: copy the dish's "Prep ahead" line, written as an action with its moment ("Take the salmon out of the freezer on Monday evening").

## 6. Showing the menu

A compact table, one row per day: day · lunch · dinner · markers (🥡 lunchbox, 🆕 new dish, ⏰ prep ahead, ♻️ leftovers). Then at most three lines: assumptions, products used up, one question if needed. No nutrition figures.

The user corrects as they like. Apply the corrections and show only the rows that changed. Aim for a short exchange.

## 7. Writing to Notion

After the user's yes:

1. Read the Menu lines already on those dates. Update them rather than creating duplicates.
2. Create the missing lines in one call: Name, Date, Meal, Dish (empty for Leftovers or Lunchbox), Status (Planned, Leftovers or Lunchbox), Servings, Extra for lunchbox, Prep ahead, Note.
3. Confirm in one line. If the shopping list module is on, offer to build the list now.
4. If the user stated a lasting preference during the exchange, propose to record it in the profile.

## 8. Never

- Never write to Notion before the user's yes.
- Never plan a Banned dish, a dish with a strict restriction, or an out-of-season dish.
- Never plan fresh products from the coming shopping on a use-up day.
- Never show calories or nutrition figures.
